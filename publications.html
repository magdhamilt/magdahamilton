Expanded 2026: Conference on Animation and Interactive Art · Linz, Austria · September 9–11, 2026 · ACM · CC BY 4.0 · Forthcoming

Abstract

This paper presents Solaris, an interactive WebGL simulation of Lem’s sentient ocean, built around a single design question: can a simulation produce the experience of encountering something whose interior stays closed to the viewer? The work treats unknowability as a property of an encounter rather than of a system, and argues that authoring it requires ontological choices made in code—in physics, communication structure, and response grammar. Four design resources produce this refusal independently of system complexity—category defeat, hidden state, timescale mismatch, and contentless channels—examined through three subsystems: a viscoelastic physics model with memory and intent terms, a gravitational labor visualization, and a mood-governed response system. A fourth subsystem, the mimoid, fails: its generative principle operates on recognizable referents, and the encounter collapses the moment a viewer names one.

Technical Report · Velvet Disruption · Zenodo · Third edition, 24 July 2026 · CC BY 4.0

Abstract

Solaris is a browser-based, real-time simulation of a planet-sized sentient ocean, built to run entirely client-side through a C++ physics core compiled to WebAssembly and rendered in WebGL. Rather than modeling the surface as water, the ocean is a Kelvin–Voigt viscoelastic gel integrated on a 128×128 grid at 60 Hz, extended with two non-standard fields: a slow memory term that lets deformation persist, and an intent term that drives motion with no external cause. A dual-sun configuration feeds a per-cell tidal potential each frame; a small fraction displaces the surface while the remainder accumulates as a stress value surfaced in the fragment shader as a subsurface glow, presenting gravitational load as visible effort. Cursor interaction is routed through a drifting mood state that reshapes the material response, and six formation types emerge and dissolve on independent schedules. This report documents the architecture, the physical model, and the rendering and interaction pipeline.
